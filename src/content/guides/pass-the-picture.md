Pass the Picture is a cooperative drawing game for two people sharing one device. The canvas belongs to both of you: on each pass, one player adds a single stroke with a finger, pen, or mouse, then the handoff screen offers the device to the other player. Unlike the competitive Pass & Play games, the drawing stays visible during the handoff — it is shared rather than secret.

## How to play

Choose 2, 3, 4, or 5 passes per player before or during a picture (3 by default). Each pass is one stroke: press and drag on the canvas in any color from the eight-color palette at the game's single stroke width, and lifting the pointer finishes the stroke and ends the pass. If a line goes wrong, **Undo last stroke** removes the newest stroke and hands that pass back to the player who drew it, so nothing is lost to a mistake.

When the final pass ends, the whole picture is shown with a **Download this picture** button that saves a PNG file straight from your browser — nothing is uploaded, and no account is involved.

## Controls

- **Drawing:** press and drag with a finger, pen, or mouse. Lifting the pointer ends the pass.
- **Colors:** the small palette of eight colors marks the chosen one; picking a color does not use your stroke.
- **Undo:** removes the newest stroke and returns that pass to its author.
- **Passes:** set passes per player before or during a picture.
- **Pass the device:** the handoff screen appears after every stroke, with the picture still visible.

Drawing itself needs a pointer: keyboard players can change color, undo strokes, and start a new picture, but strokes cannot be drawn with keys alone — the game page says so plainly rather than implying otherwise.

## Making a picture work

### Agree on a subject first

One shared word before the first stroke saves a lot of guesswork — the second player is completing an idea, not reading a mystery.

### Outline early, color later

The first passes set the shape, and later passes add detail. Early broad lines are easier to extend than a pile of small marks, so the first player does well to keep it simple and legible.

### One idea per stroke

A stroke that adds one clear thing — a line, a curve, a shape — is easier for the next player to build on than a busy mark that mixes several ideas.

## What stays on this device

The most recent record — passes and strokes drawn — stays on this device and appears in My Arcade as the single record for this game. The drawing itself is not saved anywhere unless you download it. Nothing is uploaded, and no account is involved. Clearing site data or using the Privacy page's **Clear game data** control removes the record.
