Last Token is a small take-away game for two people sharing one device. On your turn, remove one, two, or three tokens from a single pile. The catch is in the name: the player who takes the very last token loses the round.

## How to play

Each round starts from one of three presets — the three-pile 3-4-5, the longer four-pile 1-3-5-7, or the quick two-pile 3-5 — and the player who opens alternates every round. A turn removes one to three tokens from a single pile; you cannot split a turn across piles, and you cannot take more than a pile holds. The round ends the moment the last token is gone, and the player who took it loses.

## Controls

- **Mouse or touch:** every take is a labeled button — Take 1, Take 2, or Take 3 — under each pile, sized to what that pile's remaining count allows.
- **Keyboard:** the labeled take buttons work with the arrow keys plus Enter, exactly like the pointer path.
- **Pass the device:** the handoff screen appears after each take; Continue passes play on.
- **Rounds:** pick a preset before or between rounds; opening player alternates automatically.

Because a turn is always a single labeled take, the game is identical in feel for touch, pointer, and keyboard players.

## Reading the endgame

### Leave the single-token pile

A position with exactly one pile of one token is a loss for whoever must move — they have no choice but to take the last token. So the endgame goal is simple: play so that the opponent is the one facing it.

### Count single-token piles in the end

When every remaining pile holds exactly one token, the parity decides: an odd number of such piles is a loss for the player to move (they take one, leaving an even number — a win — for you), and an even number is a win for the player to move. In a long round like 1-3-5-7, you are often playing earlier moves *toward* this endgame shape.

### Think in what you leave, not what you take

Every take is really a choice about the position you hand back: how many piles remain, and how large. A take that looks small but leaves your opponent a position where every reply is bad is worth more than a take that simply removes tokens.
