NoCharge Memory Match uses a 4×4 board containing eight pairs of symbols. The goal is simple: reveal every pair while using as few moves as possible. Because the symbols are shuffled for each new board, the useful skill is not memorizing one layout—it is building a reliable way to scan any layout.

## How to play

Select one hidden card, then select a second card. A matching pair stays face up. A mismatched pair turns back over after a short pause. Each two-card attempt counts as one move, whether the cards match or not.

The game ends when all eight pairs are visible. Your lowest completed move count is saved as your best result in this browser.

## Controls

- **Mouse or touch:** select any hidden card.
- **Keyboard:** use Tab to move between cards, then press Enter or Space to reveal one.
- **New game:** reshuffles the full board and resets the current move count.

Matched cards leave the keyboard sequence, and focus moves to another available card so you can continue without returning to a pointer.

## A practical memory strategy

### Reveal new information early

During the opening turns, avoid repeatedly guessing the same unknown area. Reveal cards you have not seen so each move adds two useful positions to memory.

### Remember pairs as locations

Instead of trying to hold sixteen separate symbols in your head, connect a symbol to a simple location: “blue circle, top-right” or “white circle, third row left.” Row-and-column language is often easier to recall than an isolated picture.

### Use known matches immediately

When a newly revealed card matches a position you already remember, take that pair on the next move. Clearing known pairs reduces the number of active locations competing for attention.

### Scan in one direction

Pick a consistent pattern—left to right, top to bottom—and return to it on each board. A repeatable scan makes card positions less likely to blur together.

## What is a good move count?

A perfect game would require eight moves, one for each pair, but that would mean knowing the shuffled layout in advance. For ordinary play, improvement is more useful than a universal target. Try to beat your own best by one or two moves as your scanning method becomes more consistent.

## Where your score is stored

The best move count is saved only in local storage on the current device. It is not uploaded, shared, or connected to an account. Clearing site data or using the Privacy page’s **Clear game data** control removes it.
