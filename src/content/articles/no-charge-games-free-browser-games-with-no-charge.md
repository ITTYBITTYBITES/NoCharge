> **Bottom line:** All games in the NoCharge arcade are available at no charge and without an account or subscription. Some pages can show one labeled advertisement, and some Quiet Setup articles have optional marked affiliate links, but neither is part of a game’s rules or access.

The brand is written **NoCharge**, as one word. If you arrived looking for “no charge games,” this page explains the practical meaning behind that name rather than just repeating the phrase: what is free, what can be stored, which third parties may appear, and what the site does not promise.

## What “at no charge” includes

| Part of the site | What is available | Account needed? | What may remain in the browser? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arcade | 17 published browser games | No | A game-specific best result, progress value, preference, or recent match where documented |
| Game guides | One guide for each published game | No | Nothing required to read the guide |
| Articles and collections | Platform explanations, play notes, and reviewed game groupings | No | Nothing required to read them |
| Quiet Setup | 210 editorial setup guides across seven topics | No | Nothing required; optional Amazon links open on Amazon |
| My Arcade | A view of results already saved by this browser | No | It reads the same allowlisted local game data and does not create a profile |
| Tools | Discovery, ambient-audio, and zoom utilities | No | Current inputs are handled in the page; shared audio volume is a local preference |

Free access does not mean that every page is free of commercial material. Eligible pages can include one responsive Google AdSense banner outside gameplay. Quiet Setup does not show display ads, but some articles contain clearly marked Amazon affiliate links. Those links are optional, appear after no-purchase checks, and do not unlock games or affect scores.

## What stays on the device

NoCharge has no user-account system, cloud save, or public leaderboard. A game can store only its documented local values, such as Memory Match’s best moves, a puzzle-progress value, the Recently Played list, or one bounded Pass & Play match record. My Arcade reads those values from the current browser.

A different browser, profile, private window, or device will not automatically have the same results. Clearing browser site data can remove them. The **Clear game data** control on Privacy and My Arcade removes the project’s explicit game-data allowlist while leaving the separate analytics choice and Google consent data alone.

## Optional services and choices

NoCharge analytics is off until a visitor allows it. Advertising consent is handled separately through Google Privacy & messaging. The footer keeps controls for both choices available after the first visit. Declining optional analytics does not block the games.

The site cannot promise that every browser extension, network policy, device, or old browser will behave identically. It also does not promise uninterrupted availability or recovery of local results. The Help, Privacy, Advertising, Accessibility, and Terms pages describe those boundaries in more detail.

## How to verify the claims yourself

1. Open the [Arcade](/arcade/) in a current browser; no sign-in prompt is part of the game launch.
2. Make a meaningful move, then open [My Arcade](/my-arcade/) to see whether that game documents a saved result.
3. Review [Privacy](/privacy/) for the exact clearing boundary and the difference between game data and consent data.
4. Review [Advertising](/advertising/) for display-ad placement and affiliate-link labeling.
5. Use a game’s [complete guide](/guides/) to confirm controls, timing, and storage behavior for that title.

## Limits of the name

“NoCharge” describes access to the site’s own games; it is not a claim that internet service, electricity, hardware, mobile data, or third-party purchases cost nothing. Amazon and Google operate their own services under their own terms. No purchase is needed to use the arcade, and a paid link is never required to complete a game.

## Next action

Choose a game from the Arcade, read its controls, and play one round. If you want to inspect or remove anything the browser retained afterward, use My Arcade or the local-data controls on Privacy.
