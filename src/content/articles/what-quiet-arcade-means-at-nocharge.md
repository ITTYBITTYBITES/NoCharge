NoCharge exists to make a small part of the web easier to enter and easier to leave. The [Arcade](/arcade/) offers four browser games now: Memory Match, Word Tile Rush, Color Flip, and Beacon Lattice. A visitor can open one, understand the controls, play a short session, and move on without creating an account.

“Quiet Arcade” is a product standard, not a new game mode and not a promise that every challenge is slow.

## Start without an account

There is no registration screen, profile, password, cloud save, or social feed between the visitor and a game. Each current title runs in the page. Typical sessions range from about one to eight minutes, although nothing prevents a visitor from playing another run or working through more Beacon Lattice puzzles.

Short does not mean rushed. Memory Match and Beacon Lattice are untimed. Color Flip offers both a moving Visual mode and an untimed Turn-based mode. Word Tile Rush is intentionally timed once the first letter is selected. Quiet Arcade does **not** mean every game is untimed; it means the timing and pressure are stated plainly.

## Controls should explain themselves

Current games support touch and pointer input as well as documented keyboard operation. Controls use buttons, text labels, visible focus, and status feedback rather than requiring a visitor to infer everything from an animation. Motion and sound are restrained, and essential results do not depend on sound. The shared mute preference follows the visitor between games in the same browser.

The [Accessibility Statement](/accessibility/) describes the current target and known testing limits. It treats WCAG 2.2 AA as a target, not a certification.

## Local results, honest guides

Scores, Beacon Lattice progress, and game preferences stay in local browser storage. They are not account records and do not synchronize to another browser. The [Privacy page](/privacy/) explains what is stored and provides a narrow Clear Game Data control.

Every game also has a definitive guide. Supporting articles focus on real rules—such as Memory Match move counting, Word Tile Rush paths, Color Flip modes, and Beacon Lattice exact coverage. The editorial rule is simple: documentation must follow the runtime, not a convenient marketing version of it.

## Advertising stays outside play

Eligible pages can show one labeled Google AdSense banner near the bottom, after the main content. It does not sit inside the game viewport or game controls. Google manages advertising choices through Privacy & messaging; NoCharge’s separate analytics choice remains optional.

Quiet does not mean ad-free, disconnected from third-party services, or free of challenge. It means advertising is identified and separated, controls and policies are readable, and the site avoids adding pressure through accounts, streaks, leaderboards, or artificial rewards.

## What it does not promise

Quiet Arcade is not a claim that games improve health, memory, education, or performance. It is not a claim of perfect accessibility. It does not mean every browser or assistive-technology combination has completed manual testing. It also does not turn local storage into a cloud backup.

For the broader project rationale and contact details, read [About NoCharge](/about/). Then choose a game in the [Arcade](/arcade/) with a clear understanding of what the site does—and what it deliberately leaves out.
