Monitor calibration for browser gaming is less about color accuracy and more about reducing eye strain during long sessions. NoCharge games use dark backgrounds with modest contrast, which already helps. The useful adjustments are the ones you can make with settings you already have.

## Start with brightness

The most impactful setting is brightness. A monitor that is too bright relative to the room causes glare fatigue; one that is too dim forces your eyes to work harder to distinguish details. A reasonable starting point: match the monitor's white level to the brightness of a white piece of paper held next to the screen under your current room lighting.

Most monitors expose brightness through their on-screen display (OSD). Laptop users typically adjust via keyboard shortcuts or system settings. The goal is not a specific number — it is a relationship between the screen and the room.

## Contrast and gamma

Contrast controls the ratio between the brightest and darkest parts of the image. Too high, and shadow details disappear; too low, and everything looks washed out. Default settings are usually adequate for NoCharge games, which avoid extreme brightness ranges.

Gamma affects mid-tone brightness. If dark game elements are hard to distinguish, a slightly lower gamma (brighter mid-tones) may help without increasing overall brightness.

## Blue light and evening sessions

Blue light does not inherently damage eyes, but it can affect sleep timing when viewed in the hours before bed. Most operating systems offer a built-in night mode that shifts colors toward warm tones on a schedule:

- **Windows:** Night light (Settings > Display)
- **macOS:** Night Shift (System Settings > Displays)
- **Linux:** Most desktop environments offer a redshift or night-color option

These software solutions are free and reversible. A physical blue-light filter is an alternative for readers who prefer hardware-level filtering or who share a display with color-critical work where software tinting would interfere.

## What to check before buying anything

Before adding hardware, try these free adjustments:

1. Reduce monitor brightness to match room lighting.
2. Enable your OS night mode for evening sessions.
3. Move the monitor to avoid direct window reflections.
4. Increase browser zoom if text or game elements feel small.
5. Check that your monitor's refresh rate is set to its native value.
