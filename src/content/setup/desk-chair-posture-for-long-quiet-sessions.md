Comfortable posture for browser gaming is not fundamentally different from comfortable posture for any desk work. The useful principles are well-documented and do not require expensive equipment. Most adjustments start with what you already have.

## The basics

- **Feet flat on the floor** (or on a footrest if the chair is too high).
- **Knees roughly level with or slightly below hips.**
- **Lower back supported.** A small cushion or rolled towel behind the lumbar curve is often sufficient.
- **Shoulders relaxed, not hunched.**
- **Elbows close to the body, forearms roughly horizontal.**
- **Screen at or slightly below eye level** so the neck is not tilted up or sharply down.

## What to adjust before buying

### Chair height

Most chairs adjust up and down. If your feet don't rest flat, the chair is too high. If your knees are above your hips, it's too low. A footrest can solve a too-high chair without replacing it.

### Seat depth

If the chair seat is too deep (pressing behind the knees), move forward and add a lumbar cushion behind your back. If too shallow, no adjustment helps — but most desk chairs are deep enough for average builds.

### Backrest angle

A slight recline (100–110 degrees) reduces pressure on the lower back compared to a perfectly upright 90-degree posture. If your chair allows it, try a slight recline.

### Armrest height

Armrests should let your shoulders stay relaxed while your forearms rest lightly. Too high forces shoulders up; too low encourages leaning. If armrests aren't adjustable, removing them (if possible) may be more comfortable than fighting their position.

## Movement matters more than posture

No single posture is comfortable indefinitely. The most impactful habit for long sessions is regular position changes. Stand up, stretch, walk briefly. This matters more than the specific chair or cushion.
