> **Bottom line:** Compare stand stability, orientation, charging clearance, adjustment, and footprint while keeping handheld play as a supported option. Adjust height, distance, and browser zoom first; check VESA and stability before buying.

A stand changes where a screen rests; it does not make handheld play obsolete. NoCharge continues to support handheld browser play where a game documents touch controls. A stand may help when the device needs to stay visible while using another input, but it can also add wobble and take up space.

## Who may find a stand useful

This guide is for a reader who has identified a placement problem: the device slides, a case prop offers only one angle, the charging cable is pinched, or portrait and landscape need different support. If the device is comfortable and stable in hand, no purchase is required.

## Stability before features

Check the stand’s base width and depth against the device’s height and weight. A tall screen creates leverage, especially during direct taps. Soft contact points can reduce sliding, but their position must match the device and case. A heavy base is not automatically stable if the hinge or support lip flexes.

For touch play, imagine pressing near all four corners. A stand intended only for passive viewing may move under those inputs. Folding designs trade some rigidity for portability. Clamp designs move the footprint elsewhere but require a compatible edge and careful tightening.

## Orientation and clearance

A useful support should hold the actual device in both orientations you plan to use. In landscape, side buttons or speakers may meet the support. In portrait, the charging port often sits at the bottom; measure the connector body and cable bend, not just the port opening.

Cases change thickness, corner shape, and weight. Measure with the case attached if it normally stays on. Check camera bumps only if they touch the support. Adjustment can mean hinge angle, height, rotation, or all three; decide which axis solves the identified problem.

## Alternatives already nearby

Try the built-in case prop, a stable book support that does not cover ventilation, or a lower device angle. Avoid precarious stacks and do not place a device where it can fall or overheat. Browser zoom and orientation can also improve readability without changing physical support.

## Evidence and limits

NoCharge used editorial research to compare stand design criteria. We did not load-test hinges, verify every device fit, or personally use the search results. This is not safety, medical, or ergonomic advice, and it cannot guarantee comfort or compatibility.

## Next action

Measure device width, thickness with case, charging-connector clearance, and available footprint. Test portrait and landscape with the current support. Browse only if one measured requirement is still unmet, then verify dimensions rather than relying on a generic compatibility claim.
