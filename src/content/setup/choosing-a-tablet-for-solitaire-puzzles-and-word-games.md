Tablets offer a compelling sweet spot for browser gaming: larger, more legible card stacks and word grids than a smartphone, combined with the relaxing touch interaction of a physical surface.

Whether you enjoy daily rounds of [Klondike Solitaire](/games/klondike/), finding words in [Word Search](/games/word-search/), or drawing in [Pass the Picture](/games/pass-the-picture/), here is what to consider when choosing a tablet for casual web play.

## Screen size and aspect ratio

**8-Inch Tablets (Compact).** Weighing under 300 grams, an 8-inch tablet can be held easily with one hand while tapping cards with the other. They are ideal for reading on the couch or playing short breaks on public transit.

**10 to 11-Inch Tablets (Standard).** The standard size provides ample room for 8-column tableau games like [FreeCell](/games/freecell/) and 10×10 grids in Word Search. Cards and letters are large and distinct without straining your vision.

**Aspect Ratio (4:3 vs. 16:10).** A 4:3 or 3:2 screen ratio feels more like a physical book or card table in portrait orientation. 16:9 widescreen tablets can feel narrow and tall in portrait mode, though they work well in landscape.

## Hardware requirements for lightweight browser games

Unlike resource-heavy 3D native games, NoCharge's static web games require very little processing power. You do not need expensive flagship processors. Even budget tablets from several years ago run these titles with instant responsiveness.

## Alternatives already nearby

Before buying new hardware, check if you have an older iPad or Android tablet with an open browser. Because NoCharge requires no app store downloads or accounts, older tablets often make excellent dedicated puzzle boards.
