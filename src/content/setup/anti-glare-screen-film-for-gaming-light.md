An anti-glare screen film is a thin matte overlay that diffuses reflections. It helps when a window or lamp produces a visible bright spot on your display that cannot be eliminated by moving the display, closing a blind, or redirecting the light. It does not help when the issue is brightness, contrast, or text size.

## When it helps

- A window behind you reflects as a bright rectangle on the screen.
- An overhead light creates a hotspot that washes out dark game elements.
- You cannot move the display, the light source, or yourself.

## When it doesn't help

- The problem is overall brightness or contrast (use monitor settings).
- Text and controls feel small (use browser zoom).
- The screen is already matte (most laptop screens have a matte coating).
- The reflections come from a light you can move or block.

## Try these before buying

1. Move the monitor to avoid direct window reflections.
2. Close or angle blinds to redirect sunlight.
3. Reposition desk lamps so they don't shine on the screen.
4. Adjust monitor brightness to match the room.
5. Use a desk lamp with a directed beam instead of overhead lighting.

## What to look for

If a film is the right solution, check:

- **Exact size match.** Films are cut for specific screen sizes and aspect ratios. A mismatched film leaves gaps or overhangs.
- **Matte vs glossy.** Anti-glare films are matte. They diffuse reflections but slightly reduce perceived text sharpness.
- **Application method.** Wet-application films are more forgiving but harder to apply. Dry-application films are simpler but may trap bubbles.
- **Removability.** Some films leave residue; others use static cling and remove cleanly.
