Color temperature describes whether a light source appears warm (yellowish, like incandescent) or cool (bluish, like daylight). It is measured in Kelvin (K). For desk lighting during evening browser play, warmer light (2700–3000K) is generally more comfortable than cooler light (5000K+).

## Why it matters for evening play

Cool light suppresses melatonin production more than warm light. This is useful during daytime work when alertness matters, but counterproductive in the hours before sleep. If you play browser games in the evening, a warm desk lamp complements your OS night-mode setting by reducing blue-light exposure from the room as well as the screen.

## What to adjust

### Lamp color temperature

If your lamp offers adjustable color temperature, shift to warm (2700–3500K) for evening sessions. Fixed-temperature warm lamps are widely available and inexpensive.

### Lamp position

A desk lamp should illuminate the desk surface and keyboard without shining on the monitor screen or directly into your eyes. Position it to the side and angled downward.

### Brightness

Evening desk lighting should be bright enough to see the keyboard and desk surface but dim enough that the monitor doesn't appear to be floating in darkness. A moderate-brightness warm lamp creates a comfortable ambient level.

## Free adjustments

1. **Use your OS night mode.** This warms the screen output without changing the room lighting.
2. **Turn off overhead lights.** Overhead fluorescent or LED panels are often cool-temperature and bright. Switching to a single warm desk lamp for evening play reduces overall blue-light exposure.
3. **Dim existing lamps.** If your lamp has a dimmer, lower brightness for evening sessions.
