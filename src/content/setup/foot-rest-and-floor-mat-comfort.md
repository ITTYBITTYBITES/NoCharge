A footrest fills the gap between your feet and the floor when a chair is too high. An anti-fatigue mat softens standing on a hard floor. Both are simple tools that solve specific comfort problems — neither is universally necessary.

## When a footrest helps

Your chair is at the right height for your desk and arms, but your feet don't rest flat on the floor. This is common with fixed-height desks and shorter users. A footrest raises the floor to meet your feet, completing the support chain from feet to lower back.

A footrest also helps when you want to vary leg position during long sessions. Alternating between feet flat, one foot elevated, and ankles crossed reduces static posture fatigue.

## What to look for in a footrest

- **Height:** enough to let your feet rest flat with knees at or below hip level. Adjustable height is useful.
- **Surface:** textured or slightly angled surfaces encourage micro-movements. Flat smooth surfaces are simpler.
- **Size:** fits under your desk opening without interfering with chair movement.
- **Stability:** doesn't slide on the floor when you rest your feet on it.

## Free alternatives

A stack of books, a shoebox, or a small wooden board can serve as a footrest. The useful test is whether elevating your feet changes your comfort. If it does, a purpose-built footrest offers better ergonomics and stability. If it doesn't, no purchase is needed.

## When an anti-fatigue mat helps

You stand at your desk (sit-stand desk) for part of the day, and a hard floor causes foot or leg discomfort. An anti-fatigue mat provides slight cushioning that encourages micro-movements and reduces pressure on joints.

For seated-only desk use, a mat is not useful. A standard chair mat (for rolling chairs on carpet) is a different product.
