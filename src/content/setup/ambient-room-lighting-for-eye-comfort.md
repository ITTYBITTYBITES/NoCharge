A monitor in a dark room creates high contrast between the screen and the surrounding environment. Your pupils adjust to the average brightness of your visual field; when the screen is bright and everything else is dark, your eyes work harder to adapt when glancing away and back. Adding modest ambient light behind or around the monitor — called bias lighting — reduces this contrast.

## What bias lighting does

A light source behind the monitor illuminates the wall behind it, raising the ambient brightness around the screen without adding glare to the display surface. This reduces the contrast between the screen and its surroundings, which can reduce eye strain during long sessions.

Bias lighting does not need to be bright. A soft, warm glow is sufficient. The goal is to eliminate the "floating rectangle in darkness" effect.

## Free solutions

Before buying a dedicated bias light:

1. **Turn on a room light.** Even a dim lamp across the room provides ambient light that reduces screen-to-room contrast.
2. **Position a desk lamp behind the monitor.** A lamp aimed at the wall behind the screen acts as bias lighting.
3. **Leave a hallway light on.** Light spilling into the room from an adjacent space provides ambient illumination without direct glare.

## Dedicated bias lights

USB-powered LED strips that mount to the back of a monitor are a common dedicated solution. They draw power from the monitor's USB port (or a nearby adapter), so they turn on and off with the monitor. Warm white (2700–3500K) is comfortable for evening use.

## What to avoid

- **Light shining on the screen.** Any light source that reflects off the monitor surface adds glare, not comfort.
- **Very bright bias lighting.** The goal is modest ambient light, not illumination. Too bright defeats the purpose.
- **Colored bias lighting for work.** RGB effects are fine for aesthetics but may interfere with color-sensitive work. Warm white is the safe default.
